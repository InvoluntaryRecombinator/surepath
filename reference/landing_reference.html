It used to work backwards.

To find out whether your record disqualified you, you had to apply. To apply, you had to already be qualified — the training, the hours, the fees. In practice that meant spending a year and a tuition to learn the answer was no.

Nobody could tell you in advance. Not the school, not your officer, not a search at two in the morning.

States started answering the question first.

A number of states have recognized this and passed laws letting people with criminal records request a determination from certain licensing boards before putting their time and money on the line.

The processes are real. They are also buried in lengthy forms and scattered instructions, which is why almost nobody uses them.

SurePath is how you ask.

Plain questions instead of dense forms, and a finished packet ready to send. No account, no lawyer, no decoding.

We don't decide anything.

Licensing is handled at the state level, and the board in your state makes the final call on how your record affects your eligibility. That is exactly who this process asks. You are not getting our guess — you are getting their answer, in writing, signed.

SurePath does not give legal advice. We help you ask the question correctly.

Find your state

Before you begin, you'll need your complete criminal history — every state you've been convicted in, not just the one you're choosing. For each conviction: the date, the county, and the specific court that handled it.

If your record is long or spread across states, the reliable way to get it is an official copy from the records agency in each state. For a single charge, the county clerk's office for that courthouse can usually confirm the details.
